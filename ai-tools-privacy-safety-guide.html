How to Use AI Tools Safely: Privacy and Security Tips

AI tools can help with writing, analysis, coding, and everyday work, but the information you submit may be sensitive. A few practical habits can reduce privacy, security, and reliability risks.

Understand the tool before entering data

AI services do not all handle data in the same way. Before using one for work, review its privacy notice, data controls, retention terms, account settings, and any organizational policy that applies. Do not assume that a consumer account has the same protections as a business or enterprise service. If the terms are unclear, keep sensitive data out.

Never paste secrets into an unapproved AI tool

Avoid submitting passwords, authentication tokens, private keys, recovery codes, payment details, confidential customer records, unpublished business plans, or personal data unless the service and your organization explicitly permit that use. Redact logs and documents first. Replace real identifiers with synthetic examples when troubleshooting or asking for help.

Use data minimization and access controls

Share only the information needed to complete the task. Remove names, addresses, account numbers, and other identifiers where possible. Use multi-factor authentication, unique passwords, least-privilege access, and approved accounts. For teams, define who may use AI tools, which data classes are allowed, and how outputs should be reviewed.

Treat AI output and external content as untrusted

An AI-generated email, script, summary, or recommendation can contain errors or unsafe instructions. Review links and attachments before opening them. Inspect code before running it, especially if it downloads files, changes permissions, accesses credentials, or deletes data. Never allow a chatbot to override normal security checks just because it says a step is necessary.

Watch for impersonation and synthetic media

AI can make it easier to create convincing text, images, voices, or videos. Verify unusual requests for money, gift cards, credentials, or urgent account changes through a separate, known contact method. A familiar voice or polished message is not sufficient proof of identity. Organizations should establish verification steps for payments and sensitive changes.

Build a simple AI safety checklist

Before each task, ask: Is this tool approved? Does the prompt contain sensitive information? Can I anonymize the input? Will I verify the output? Could the action affect a real system or person? For higher-risk organizational use, document the use case, assign responsibility, test for failure modes, and monitor the system over time.

What organizations can do

Organizations can classify data, publish approved-use guidance, evaluate vendors, limit access, train staff, and maintain incident-reporting procedures. AI risk management should be proportionate to the potential impact and reviewed as the technology or use case changes. NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework and Generative AI Profile provide structured resources for this work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I paste company documents into a free AI chatbot?

Only if your organization permits it and the service’s terms and settings meet its requirements. When unsure, do not upload confidential documents.

Do AI tools store every prompt?

Practices vary by provider, product, settings, and account type. Read the current service privacy documentation rather than assuming a universal rule.

Can AI-generated code be unsafe?

Yes. Review, test, and scan code before deployment; do not expose production secrets or grant unnecessary permissions.

How can I verify an urgent request from a manager?

Use a separate trusted channel or established approval process, especially for payments, credentials, and account changes.
